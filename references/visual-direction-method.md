# Visual Direction Method

A direction is a usable design thesis for a real deck, not a color card or decorative mood board.

## Required Content

Each direction must contain:

1. communication thesis and narrative emphasis;
2. representative slides shown at readable, full-size scale;
3. typography character and hierarchy behavior;
4. image, chart, and diagram behavior;
5. layout rhythm and expected silhouette variation;
6. motion logic, including when motion should be absent;
7. mature references actually inspected, or an explicit local/original fallback label;
8. trade-offs for the intended audience and decision.

Directions must differ materially in narrative emphasis, composition, typography, imagery, and rhythm. Palette changes alone do not count as separate directions.

## Reference Use

- Prefer user-provided references, existing deck templates, trusted local libraries, and mature open-source or public design work.
- Inspect the real source before claiming it informed a direction.
- Adapt layout logic, hierarchy, pacing, or media treatment semantically.
- Follow licenses and attribution requirements.
- Never copy another brand's logo, identity system, exclusive imagery, biography, profile material, or proprietary template.

If external reference access fails, record `missing evidence`. Use inspected local resources or clearly label the direction as original exploration.

## Presentation Rules

- Show one representative slide at a useful scale before showing another.
- Do not shrink a full deck into a thumbnail collage and ask the user to infer typography or hierarchy.
- Do not use palette swatches as proof of a complete direction.
- Do not reuse one generic card-grid layout across every direction.
- Do not add decorative spinning, floating, looping, or cinematic motion without a narrative purpose.
- Do not treat the selected test direction as a fixed standard for future decks.

## Selection Handoff

Record what the user chose and what they rejected. Convert the selected direction into task-specific design decisions before invoking `Presentations`; do not paste the preview itself into the final deck.
