# Quality Gates

Technical validity and visual quality are separate gates. Both must pass.

## Typography And Fit

- Select typography for the audience, language, display environment, content density, and chosen direction.
- Do not hard-code a universal font, fixed size scale, or palette in this standard.
- Follow the active template when one exists; otherwise follow the current `Presentations` readability minimums.
- Shorten copy or choose another layout before reducing type.
- Reject unexpected wrapping, especially a one-line title or banner wrapping to two lines.

## Composition

- Use one coherent visual system while varying silhouettes to fit the narrative.
- Avoid repetitive UI-card grids, pills, tabs, decorative controls, and nested panels unless explaining an actual interface.
- Balance information density and whitespace; neither crowding nor decorative emptiness is acceptable.
- Keep alignment, spacing, page markers, source treatment, and hierarchy consistent.

## Assets

- Use assets that reveal the actual product, place, process, object, or evidence when inspection matters.
- Check resolution, crop, framing, color consistency, and source traceability at full-slide size.
- Replace blurry, stretched, distorted, dark, or weakly framed media.
- Do not reuse the same image repeatedly unless it is intentionally a background or structural motif.

## Motion

Motion is justified only when it clarifies sequence, hierarchy, causality, comparison, state change, or attention. Reject decorative spinning, floating, looping, bouncing, and purposeless transitions. Missing motion is not a defect when static composition communicates more clearly.

## Review Sequence

1. Render every final slide.
2. Inspect every slide individually at full size.
3. Check title wrapping, clipping, overlap, density, hierarchy, spacing, crop, resolution, contrast, evidence, and audience safety.
4. Inspect a montage only for rhythm, silhouette variation, and narrative continuity.
5. Run the `Presentations` overflow and technical checks.
6. Revise every failed item, re-render, and inspect the affected slide plus its neighbors.

## Failure Rules

- A technical pass cannot override a failed visual review.
- A beautiful slide cannot excuse an unsupported claim or privacy violation.
- A direction board, screenshot, or montage is not the final deck.
- A low-quality asset must be replaced or removed; it is not fixed by blur, darkness, or cropping away the subject.
- A cramped slide must be rewritten or recomposed; it is not fixed by unreadably small type.
